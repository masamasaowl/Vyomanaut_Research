### Literature 1

**Name:**
Blockchain-based multi-user dynamic verifiable searchable encryption for secure data storage and query on malicious cloud server

**Publisher:**
Journal of Systems Architecture, February 2026

**Authors:**
Chunhua JinWanru LuJian Weng

**Abstract:**
Dynamic Searchable Symmetric Encryption (DSSE) is a prominent research area that enables users to search and update encrypted data stored in the cloud, thereby preserving data privacy. However, due to key distribution challenges, most existing DSSE schemes are primarily designed for single-user scenarios, which limits the broader applicability of searchable encryption. Moreover, assuming that cloud servers and third-party auditors (TPA) are semi-honest is overly optimistic, as TPAs are often difficult to supervise and may collude with cloud servers to forge verification information. In order to address the above issues, we propose a blockchain-based multi-user dynamic verifiable searchable encryption scheme. In our design, only the indexes constructed using a Cuckoo filter are stored on the blockchain, significantly reducing on-chain storage overhead. The core algorithm is fully implemented through chaincode, ensuring honest execution of all operations and preventing potential collusion between TPAs and cloud servers. To address key distribution, we employ the Diffie–Hellman key exchange protocol to securely generate user-specific keys. Furthermore, we introduce a novel verification mechanism that integrates the Cuckoo filter with a Merkle hash tree to enhance verifiability. Finally, we conduct a comprehensive security analysis and implement a system prototype on Hyperledger Fabric. Experimental results demonstrate that our scheme achieves excellent performance in terms of both storage efficiency and computational overhead.

### Literature 2

**Name:**
TMAS: A threshold multi-auditor auditing scheme for weakly trusted cloud–fog collaboration

**Publisher:**
Computers & SecurityNovember 2026

**Authors:**
Hui TianHaiju WangHanyu Quan

**Abstract:**
Cloud–fog collaboration supports low-latency and scalable data processing in the Industrial Internet. In this setting, data integrity auditing faces compound threats. The cloud may be untrusted. Fog nodes are weakly trusted and may fail or be compromised. Many existing schemes rely on a single node to generate tags and verify proofs. This creates a single point of failure and misbehavior. They also provide limited protection against cloud–fog collusion. To address these issues, we propose TMAS, a threshold multi-auditor data integrity auditing scheme for weakly trusted cloud–fog collaboration. In TMAS, a fog cluster performs a trusted initialization to distribute signing key shares via verifiable secret sharing (VSS). During operation, fog auditors collaboratively generate tags, verify proofs, and issue audit results in a threshold manner, allowing the data owner to verify that at least t auditors endorsed the result. Furthermore, we bind each audit challenge to a round identifier and embed the challenge digest into the signed result. This mechanism ensures freshness and recomputability, thereby resisting replay attacks and workflow manipulation. In addition, we construct a threshold-signature-based verifiable random beacon to rotate the coordinator each round. This improves availability without heavy consensus. Theoretical analysis proves the scheme’s correctness, auditing soundness, fallback liveness, cloud–fog collusion resistance, and resistance to replay attacks and workflow manipulation. Experiments show that TMAS introduces only about 5% additional tag-generation overhead over BAscina and completes proof generation and verification for 1,000 challenged blocks within about 1.83 s and 2.03 s, respectively, demonstrating practical scalability for large-scale cloud–fog auditing.

### Literature 3

**Name:**
PATD: Privacy-preserving auditing and transparent deduplication in UAV cloud storage

**Publisher:**
Computers & SecurityJuly 2026

**Authors:**
Chengyuan XuLibo FengYimin Yu

**Abstract:**
Existing integrity auditing and encrypted deduplication schemes for Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) cloud storage still do not simultaneously achieve public verifiability, low communication overhead, and low computation overhead in large-batch verification scenarios; moreover, their deduplication outcomes lack transparency. To address these issues, this paper presents a novel scheme named PATD for privacy-preserving auditing and transparent deduplication in UAV cloud storage. By combining efficient homomorphic authenticators with Merkle trees, PATD achieves a low-overhead and verifiable cloud storage auditing process. First, we propose an integrity verification algorithm based on polynomial commitments and aggregated proofs, reducing both verification bandwidth and computation, which suits bandwidth-constrained edge environments. Second, we design a bidirectional ownership attestation and cross-tenant sharing protocol that enforces uploaders possess ownership and ensures the cloud-side owner list is verifiable, enabling consistent governance across collaborating organizations. Third, we introduce a Merkle-tree-based transparency mechanism for deduplication that cryptographically binds deduplication counters and owner sets and makes them publicly verifiable while hiding file contents from the third-party auditor, preventing the cloud from under-reporting or misreporting and achieving both auditability and privacy protection. Finally, under a formal security model this paper proves the confidentiality and soundness of PATD, implements the protocol, and evaluates its performance. Compared with representative schemes, the performance overhead of PATD is reduced by 22.3% to 56.5%.

### Literature 5

**Name:**
CIA: A Collaborative Integrity Auditing Scheme for Cloud Data With Multi-Replica on Multi-Cloud Storage Providers

**Publisher:**
IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems ( Volume: 34, Issue: 1, 01 January 2023)

**Authors:**
Tengfei Li; Jianfeng Chu; Liang Hu

**Abstract:**
The emergence of cloud storage has solved many pain points of the traditional storage model. However, the issue of cloud storage data integrity - whether the cloud storage provider has kept the data intact - has raised concerns about cloud storage. Integrity auditing of cloud storage data allows users to know the integrity of the outsourced data without downloading it in its entirety. However it is not enough to know its integrity. Multi-replicas, as a common means of redundancy, improves the reliability of cloud storage data. And storing multi-replicas on multi-cloud storage providers (CSPs) enhances this feature. In a multi-replica multi-CSPs scenario, if the independence of CSPs is given full play and auditing is performed among CSPs, not only the introduction of third party auditor (TPA) can be eliminated, but also the tag generation, which is a huge computational overhead, can be removed. Inspired by this, in this paper we propose a new model for remote data integrity auditing: CIA (Collaborative Integrity Auditing). In addition to the reduction in computational overhead, the proposed scheme provides unprecedented support for free data blocking. The proposed scheme employs only hash functions in the calculation, which has a negligible computational overhead compared to the traditional bilinear pairing-based schemes. Theoretical analysis and experimental results show that the proposed scheme provides high efficiency and flexibility with security assurance, and can be used as a lightweight alternative to traditional remote data integrity auditing schemes in multi-replica multi-CSP scenarios.

Real-time privacy-preserved auditing for shared outsourced data

**Publisher:**
Computer Standards & Interfaces, March 2025

**Authors:**
Fengmei TangYumei LiBingbing Li

**Abstract:**
Health providers need to share patient information across healthcare networks efficiently and securely to improve medical and health services. Timely data synchronization among relevant parties is crucial for effectively containing and preventing the worsening of the condition. However, ensuring rapid information sharing while maintaining the security of sensitive patient data remains a pressing concern. In this paper, we introduce a cloud storage integrity auditing scheme that can protect auditors from procrastinating and preserve the privacy of sensitive information. Our proposed system requires healthcare institutions to encrypt sensitive patient data before uploading it to the cloud. It mandates the use of a data sanitizer for the secure processing of encrypted data blocks. Leveraging the time-sensitive nature of blockchain technology, healthcare institutions can monitor auditor compliance within the allotted validation timeframe. We conducted comprehensive security analysis and performance evaluations to demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of our solution in addressing the challenges of secure and timely cloud storage in healthcare settings.

### Literature 10

**Name:**
Privacy-Preserving Data Auditing with Efficient Corrupted Data Locating for Cloud Computing

**Publisher:**
IEEE INFOCOM 2025 - IEEE Conference on Computer Communications Workshops (INFOCOM WKSHPS), 19-19 May 2025

**Authors:**
Wenying Zheng; Tianqi Zhou; Zakirul Alam Bhuiyan; Jian Shen

**Abstract:**
Cloud computing enables on-demand access to a shared pool for computing, storage, networks, services, and applications. As an efficient provable data possession method, data auditing protocol allows a client to verify the integrity of the stored data on an untrusted server. Whereas, with the increasing threat of cyber security, data auditing protocols face increasing risks of privacy leakage. In particular, the challenge requests from the auditor to server may expose the addresses of the outsourced data, thereby threatening the security of the stored data. On the other hand, efficient corrupted data locating has always been the main bottleneck that restricts the performance of data auditing protocols. In this paper, we design a privacy-preserving data auditing protocol with efficient corrupted data locating. First, the indistinguishability obfuscation (iO) technique is introduced in the sampling process thus protecting the addresses of sensitive data. Second, an efficient corrupted data locating method is designed via the binary search algorithm, which could locate the corrupted data in logarithmic complexity. Third, memory factors are introduced to adaptively adjust the sampling range, thereby facilitating auditing with higher accuracy. Finally, we conduct a performance evaluation to prove the privacy-preserving, corruption locating, and adaptive sampling selection properties of the proposed protocol. The evaluation results show that the proposed protocol is superior to relevant existing works.
